**Background**

_For patients:_ Access to dental care remains challenging for many individuals due to busy lifestyles, long travel distances, limited availability of dentists, prolonged waiting times, and unnecessary clinic visits for minor dental conditions. Many common problems, such as dentin hypersensitivity, early dental caries, minor oral ulcers, and routine follow-up consultations, often do not require an immediate in-person visit.

_For Dentists:_ Crowded waiting rooms with minor complaints, emergency walk-ins disrupting schedules, poor follow-up documentation.

A dentist’s time is extremely valuable, yet they frequently spend 15 to 20 minutes consulting with patients who only have dentin hypersensitivity or minor ulcers that don't require immediate chair time. Meanwhile, a patient with a true dental emergency—like facial swelling—might be stuck waiting because the clinic didn't know their condition's severity until they walked through the door. Furthermore, once a patient leaves, follow-up documentation is often fragmented or lost.

Telemedicine has become a well-established component of healthcare, with digital consultation platforms enabling millions of patients to receive timely medical advice remotely. However, the adoption of teledentistry has been comparatively limited. Unlike general medicine, dentistry still relies heavily on in-person consultations even for conditions that can be initially assessed through patient history, clinical photographs, and radiographic investigations. This gap presents an opportunity to develop an advanced teledentistry platform that enhances access to dental care while improving clinical efficiency.

Current dental practice also faces challenges in appointment scheduling. Patients frequently wait for long periods because the severity of their condition is unknown until they arrive at the clinic. Additionally, follow-up documentation is often incomplete, making it difficult to monitor treatment progress over time.

A structured, asynchronous teledentistry platform can provide early assessment, improve patient education, optimize appointment scheduling, maintain comprehensive digital follow-up records, and reduce unnecessary clinic visits.

*Problem Statement*

- Unnecessary travel for minor dental complaints.
- Long waiting times due to inefficient appointment scheduling.
- Lack of structured pre-examination before the clinic visit.
- Poor documentation of follow-up progress.
- Limited access to dental specialists in rural and underserved areas.

**Proposed Innovation**

A comprehensive, asynchronous teledentistry platform that enables patients to undergo a structured, standardized initial dental assessment before visiting the clinic. The platform relies on guided patient data entry and standardized clinical workflows to ensure high-quality information reaches the dentist.

**Patient Workflow**
1. Register on the secure digital platform.
2. Upload standardized intraoral photographs covering all tooth surfaces and oral soft tissues. The platform utilizes an on-screen visual guide (e.g., anatomical overlays and camera framing prompts) to ensure patients capture clear, correctly angled, and well-lit images.
3. Enter structured symptoms, including:
- Chief complaint
- Pain score (0–10)
- Duration of symptoms
- Sensitivity to hot, cold, or sweets(Yes/no)
- Swelling, bleeding, or mobility
4. Complete a standardized medical and dental history questionnaire with built-in logic to flag high-risk systemic conditions (e.g., diabetes, bisphosphonate use, bleeding disorders).

**Clinical Assessment**
1. The dentist reviews the submitted digital dossier and:
2. Evaluates the uploaded oral images using high-resolution digital viewers with zoom and contrast-adjustment tools.
3. Identifies possible causes of the complaint based on clinical expertise.
4. Provides oral health education and preventive advice via secure messaging or video call.
5. Determines whether an in-person visit is immediately required.
6. Recommends appropriate investigations.

The patient is provided with a digital prescription and referred to a nearby partnered diagnostic imaging center for the radiographic examination.

**Treatment Planning**
Based on the clinical information and radiographs, the dentist:
- Establishes a provisional diagnosis.
- Explains the probable cause of the condition.
- Provides preventive measures and oral hygiene guidance.
- Discusses available treatment options.
- Estimates treatment duration and the expected number of visits.

**Appointment Optimization**

Patients are categorized according to urgency using standardized clinical triage protocols based on their submitted symptoms and the dentist's review:
- Emergency (e.g., facial swelling, severe trauma)
- Urgent (e.g., acute irreversible pulpitis pain)
- Routine (e.g., asymptomatic caries, restorative needs)
- Preventive consultation (e.g., scaling, fluoride application)
- Follow-up (e.g., post-operative checks)

This allows priority-based appointment scheduling and treatment time estimation, reducing waiting times and improving clinic workflow.

**Follow-up Management**
The platform maintains a comprehensive, HIPAA/DPDP-compliant digital record of:
- Sequential intraoral photographs.
- Pain score progression.
- Radiographic history.
- Clinical notes.
- Treatment progress.
- Patient compliance.
- Outcome assessment.

This creates a longitudinal digital dental record that supports continuity of care and long-term monitoring.

**Expected Benefits**

- Reduced unnecessary dental visits.
- Improved access to dental consultation in remote and underserved regions.
- Shorter waiting times through pre-assessment.
- Better appointment scheduling based on clinical priority.
- Standardized digital documentation and medico-legal protection.
- Improved patient awareness and preventive care.
- Continuous monitoring through structured follow-up records.















**Market Context**

Telemedicine has become an established model of healthcare delivery worldwide. Platforms such as _"Practo", "Apollo 24|7", and "Tata 1mg"_ in India enable patients to consult physicians remotely, receive prescriptions, schedule appointments, and obtain follow-up care without unnecessary hospital visits. Similarly, several countries have adopted dedicated teledentistry platforms such as "TeleDent (MouthWatch)", "Dentistry.One", and "Dentulu", which support remote dental consultations, patient triage, image sharing, and care coordination.

Despite these advancements, teledentistry has not achieved the same level of adoption as telemedicine, particularly in regions like India. Most dental consultations still begin with an in-person visit, even when an initial assessment could be performed using a structured patient history, clinical photographs, and appropriate radiographic investigations. This highlights a significant opportunity to develop a comprehensive, workflow-driven teledentistry platform that improves access to oral healthcare, optimizes clinical operations, reduces unnecessary clinic visits, and enhances continuity of care through structured digital follow-up.
